Final Draft 13

The industry standard. Not the most convenient and not the cheapest - but its files are what producers expect.

9.2/10
$199.99 - one-time purchase ($249.99 - regular price on the official site)Windows / macOS / iPad / iPhoneFDX - native format30-day trial

Final Draft automatically formats and paginates your script to the Hollywood standard as you type. The app's strength is not the editor itself, but the production built around it: Beat Board for structure, Outline Editor, revision tracking with colored pages, production reports, and page locking.

The weak spots are well known too: the interface looks older than its competitors', collaboration lags well behind cloud editors, and major upgrades are paid - $99-129 - so over 5-7 years a "one-time purchase" costs more than an Arc Studio subscription.

Pros

  • Accepted everywhere: studios, agents, contests
  • Best revision and production-report tools
  • Beat Board and structure inside a single file
  • Stable pagination - one page equals one minute of screen time

Cons

  • Highest entry price
  • Paid major upgrades
  • Collaboration weaker than cloud competitors
  • No Linux or Android version
Verdict: buy it when your scripts genuinely go into production or a studio explicitly asks for it. For a first draft, its power is overkill.

Clotho.pro - the structure pick

Not just an editor, but a "board + script" system: you see the whole story at once, not only the page you are on.

9.0/10
Free plan availableFree - $0 forever; Pro - $9.99/month + tax or $90/year + tax ($7.50/month on annual billing)Pro - FDX export and co-writerWeb - runs in the browserFDX import on any planStudents - Pro free until graduation

The core idea. In Clotho, the board and the script are one story in two views. Create a scene card on the board and the scene is already in the script. Drag a card and the script rebuilds itself. Acts with readiness tracking, scene cards with storylines and timing, filters by character or storyline: you can isolate one thread and check whether it sags for 20 minutes.

The editor. Classic industry-standard formatting, hotkeys for every element, a scene navigator with live timing, and version history. This is a full editor, not a "planner on the side".

Analytics - what Final Draft doesn't have. A script dashboard: dialogue-to-action ratio, a pacing map, INT/EXT, day/night, text density, budget indicators. Plus structure recommendations: the app notices when a storyline goes quiet. An example from the site: "The protagonist is absent for 21 minutes straight (scenes 12-18). If the story is about his journey, that gap weakens engagement." This is not AI writing for you; it is a tool that shows your blind spots.

Collaboration and files. On Pro you can add one co-writer - viewer, commenter, or editor - who only needs a free account. Importing .fdx is available on any plan: Clotho itself breaks a Final Draft file down into a board, characters, and structure. Exporting .fdx and Fountain is on Pro; PDF is on any plan. A separate plus, as stated by the developer: your texts are not used to train AI and are not shared with third parties. Students get Pro free until graduation - enrolled film, screenwriting or media students apply with a university email, and after graduation Pro stays at half price for a year.

Pros

  • Board and script stay in sync - no need to track structure separately in Notion or on sticky notes
  • Analytics and alerts for sagging storylines and a missing protagonist
  • FDX import automatically breaks a finished project into structure
  • Free start; partners - NYISA and GEMS Vancouver
  • Privacy: no AI training on your texts

Cons

  • FDX and Fountain export - Pro only
  • One co-writer on Pro - too few for a large writers' room
  • No native desktop apps - browser only
  • Smaller ecosystem and community than Final Draft
Verdict: the most interesting option of 2026 for writers whose problem is not dialogue but architecture: long stories, multiple storylines, TV series. If you lose characters by act three, start with Clotho.
For: feature and TV writers, and anyone who builds structure before dialogueclotho.pro

Fade In - a professional editor with no subscription for Windows, Mac, and Linux

A professional editor with no subscription and no Hollywood markup. The choice for writers who count their money.

8.8/10
$79.95 - one-time, updates includedWindows / macOS / Linux / iOS / AndroidFree demo - PDF with a watermark

Fade In does almost everything Final Draft does - formatting, revisions, page locking, production edits, organization, and navigation - but costs a third as much and even runs on Linux, where its competitors have nothing. The interface is cleaner and faster, FDX files open and save correctly, so a producer on Final Draft won't notice the difference.

What's missing: cloud collaboration on the level of WriterDuet and visual structure analytics on the level of Clotho. This is an editor for a solo writer who keeps the structure in their head or in a separate file.

Pros

  • Best price-to-professional-features ratio
  • Widest platform support, including Linux
  • Free updates, not paid versions every cycle
  • Full FDX compatibility

Cons

  • No real-time collaborative editing
  • Fewer tutorials and a smaller community
  • Demo version watermarks PDF exports
Verdict: if you write alone and want "like Final Draft, only cheaper" - this is it. A rational purchase for years.
For: solo writers, indie film, writers on Linuxfadeinpro.com

Arc Studio Pro

The most modern and friendly interface in the genre. Cloud, outlining, and collaboration out of the box.

8.9/10
Free - 2 scriptsPro - $99 per yearWeb / Mac / iOS

Arc Studio is built around the idea that structure comes before pages: beats, outline, and cards are linked to the text, formatting never breaks, and collaborative editing and comments work like Google Docs. For a beginner, it has the lowest barrier to entry: the app suggests the format and doesn't scare you with production jargon.

The downside of the subscription: over 3 years you pay about as much as for Final Draft, and if you cancel you lose the Pro features. On the free plan, PDFs go out with a watermark.

Pros

  • Best modern interface and onboarding
  • Strong outlining and collaboration
  • Free start with no time limit
  • FDX export - producers will accept the file

Cons

  • Subscription: no cheaper than Final Draft over time
  • No full Windows app - web-first
  • Watermark on free PDFs
Verdict: the best start for beginners and indie teams who care about outlining and the cloud. Pros with production revisions will still end up in Final Draft.
For: beginners, indie writers, small teams

WriterDuet and WriterSolo

The benchmark for collaborative writing: two or three writers in one document, in real time, from the browser.

8.7/10
Free - 3 projectsPlus - $7.49 per month on annual billingWeb / desktop / mobileWriterSolo - free solo version

WriterDuet solves one job better than anyone: co-writing. Every change is visible, with built-in comments, chat, and even video calls; edit history is never lost, and read-only links are handy for sending to a producer for notes. For writers' rooms and writing pairs, it is still the de facto standard among cloud tools.

WriterSolo is the same base for a solo writer, free and with no project limit - it runs in the browser and as a free desktop app for Windows, Mac and Linux, and the desktop app works offline. The trade-off: production tools and structure analytics are shallower than Final Draft and Clotho. On WriterDuet, real-time co-writing needs a Plus plan or higher for the project owner: free users can share their scripts read-only, or co-write in a paid user's script.

Pros

  • Best real-time collaborative editing
  • Runs in the browser - nothing to install
  • WriterSolo is completely free for solo work, including a desktop app that works offline
  • FDX, Fountain, and PDF export

Cons

  • Free Duet is limited to three projects, and real-time co-writing needs Plus for the project owner
  • Structure analytics weaker than Clotho's
  • Production reports simpler than Final Draft's
Verdict: if your script isn't written alone, start here. Alone, take the free WriterSolo and don't pay until you hit the limits.
For: co-writers, writers' rooms, remote teams

Celtx

Not just the script, but all of pre-production in one place: from the page to the shooting schedule.

8.1/10
Writer - $14.99 per monthWeb / iOS / AndroidScript + production pack

Celtx started as a free editor and grew into a cloud production hub: the script is linked to breakdowns, a prop catalog, scheduling, and budgeting tools. For a small team shooting its own film, it removes the pain of stitching five different apps together.

As a pure editor, Celtx is weaker than the leaders on this list: formatting and revisions trail Final Draft and Fade In, exact prices are often only visible after signing up, and the subscription stings for a solo writer who doesn't need the production modules.

Pros

  • Script and pre-production in one system
  • Cloud and collaboration
  • Handy for short films and independent shoots

Cons

  • As an editor - average, not the best on the list
  • Subscription with no one-time option
  • Pricing is opaque until you sign up
Verdict: take it for the production, not the text. If you are both the writer and the producer of your project, Celtx pays off. If you only write, you'll overpay.
For: indie productions, short-film teams

Highland Pro

Minimalism from working screenwriter John August. You write in Fountain and the app assembles a beautiful page on its own.

8.5/10
$59.99 per year or $9.99 per month - subscriptionMac and iOS onlyFountain - native format

Highland strips away everything extra: you write plain text in Fountain markup, and the editor turns it into a properly formatted script on the fly. No panels, no distracting buttons, no production noise. For writers who think in text rather than cards, this is the fastest and calmest draft.

The limitation is obvious: Apple ecosystem only. No Windows, no cloud collaboration, and production features are deliberately minimal - many writers still do final revision work in Final Draft.

Pros

  • Cleanest, most distraction-free writing process
  • Native macOS app, very fast
  • Subscription - $59.99 per year or $9.99 per month
  • Fountain files will outlive any app

Cons

  • Mac and iOS only
  • No collaboration
  • Minimal production tools
Verdict: the ideal draft for a Mac user who values speed and quiet. For production, keep an FDX export nearby.
For: Mac users, Fountain fans, and minimalists

Scrivener

Not a screenwriting editor in the pure sense, but a research workhorse. Strong where there is a lot of material.

8.3/10
$59.99 - one-timeWindows / Mac / iOSScreenplay template + compile to FDX

Scrivener is loved by adaptation writers and builders of big worlds: research, interviews, location photos, scene versions, and notes sit next to the script, and the corkboard helps rearrange pieces. But pagination and production revisions aren't native here, the interface is dense and takes getting used to, and collaboration is practically absent. This is a tool for preparation and a first big draft, not for final delivery.

Story Architect (STARC)

A free, open-source studio for the whole story - script, characters, world, and plan in one project.

8.0/10
Free foreverWin / Mac / Linux / iOS / AndroidOpen source (GPLv3)

Story Architect (STARC) is the successor of KIT Scenarist, built by the same team: a free desktop studio that keeps the script, the characters, the locations, and the world in one project. Unlimited projects and documents, no watermarks, no expiry. It writes screenplays, TV scripts, novels, comics, stage plays, and audio plays, and one project can hold a whole series with all its episodes sharing characters and locations.

The free version already covers the core: the script editor with industry-standard formatting, character and location profiles with a relations graph, a card board linked to the script, statistics, and a cover generator. Import and export run through FDX, PDF, DOCX, and Fountain, and projects live on your own machine, so it works offline. Mind maps, the corkboard, the timeline, and the AI assistant sit behind the paid Pro tier, and real-time collaboration needs the paid TEAM plan.

Pros

  • Completely free with unlimited projects
  • Screenplay plus planning in one app
  • Works offline, files stay local
  • FDX, PDF, DOCX, and Fountain export

Cons

  • Still pre-1.0 (version 0.8.X in development)
  • Steep learning curve, less polished interface
  • Planning and AI tools need the Pro tier
Verdict: take it if you want a free studio that plans as well as it writes. For a pure editor with no learning curve, WriterSolo or Fade In are faster to start.
For: writers planning series, novels, and big worlds on a $0 budget

Trelby

Free, open-source screenwriting - no subscription, no watermark, no limits.

7.9/10
Free $0 foreverWin / Mac / LinuxOpen source (GPL)

Trelby is a free desktop screenwriter, community-maintained as version 2.4. It does one job and does it cleanly: it enforces screenplay format and pagination as you type, with auto-completion, spell checking, and a character name database of over 200,000 names. Scene, location, character, and dialogue reports come built in, and you can compare two versions of a script to see what changed.

Import covers formatted text, FDX, Celtx, Fountain, Adobe Story, and Fade In files; export covers PDF, FDX, RTF, HTML, and Fountain, with a built-in PDF generator. There are no locked features and no watermark on exports. The trade-off is age: the interface is dated, there is no collaboration or cloud sync, and on Windows and Mac it installs via pip rather than a native installer.

Pros

  • Completely free, no limits, no watermark
  • Works fully offline, files stay local
  • Clean PDF and FDX export
  • Reports and script comparison built in

Cons

  • Dated interface
  • No collaboration or cloud sync
  • Windows and Mac install via pip, not a native installer
Verdict: take it if you want a free offline editor and nothing else. If you also want planning tools, Story Architect gives you more for the same $0.
For: zero-budget solo writers on Windows and Linux

That one line, from Clotho's own site, is the whole comparison: Final Draft is the industry-standard editor, Clotho is a board, an editor and analytics in one app. Both write proper screenplays. They solve different problems.

Clotho - pros and cons

Pros: free to start, and the free plan already includes the board, the editor, FDX import and PDF export. Works on Linux through the browser, where Final Draft does not exist at all. The board and the script are genuinely one thing - you fix a scene once instead of in two tools. The analytics answer questions Final Draft never asks, like which character vanished for twenty minutes. Students get Pro free until graduation.

Cons: web-only, so no offline desktop app - you need an internet connection to write. A younger product with a smaller community and fewer tutorials than Final Draft. No production reports or revision pages for shooting. FDX export needs Pro - importing your Final Draft file is free, taking a clean .fdx back out is paid.

Final Draft - pros and cons

Pros: the industry standard. If a producer or studio asks for a file, they mean Final Draft, and its revision workflow - colored pages, locked pages, production reports - is what a script going into production needs. Beat Board and Story Map are proven planning tools. One-time purchase, no subscription for the desktop app itself.

Cons: $199.99 upfront and no free version - you pay before you know whether you like it. No Linux version. No connected story analytics: nothing tells you a storyline went quiet. Cloud collaboration costs extra through the Suite subscription.

Verdict

First script, solo writer, building the story from scratch - start with Clotho. It costs nothing to try, it works on any system including Linux, and the board plus analytics help exactly where first scripts go wrong: structure. Student - same answer, Pro is free until graduation.

Script going into production, or a producer who explicitly wants Final Draft files - buy Final Draft and don't think twice. Its revision and production tools are the reason it costs $199.99.

And the two are not mutually exclusive: build the story in Clotho, then export a clean .fdx on Pro and deliver it in Final Draft. Import is free on every Clotho plan, so you can also bring an existing Final Draft project into Clotho and see its structure on the board.

Who should choose which

  • Clotho: first script, solo writer, student, Linux user, writer who loses track of storylines and characters, anyone who wants to try before paying.
  • Final Draft: working writer delivering to studios, script entering production, writer who needs revision pages and production reports, anyone whose producer asked for Final Draft by name.
  • Both: build in Clotho, deliver in Final Draft.

Note: this comparison is based on official pricing and feature pages (finaldraft.com, clotho.pro, checked November 2026), not on an independent hands-on test of Clotho.

WriterSolo - the best free editor, full stop. No project limit, no watermark, no expiry. It writes proper screenplays and exports clean FDX and PDF. It runs in the browser and as a free desktop app for Windows, Mac and Linux, and the desktop app works offline. The only thing it does not do is co-writing in real time - for that, its paid sibling WriterDuet gives you 3 projects free.

Clotho.pro - the best free plan for structure. The free plan already includes the board, the editor, FDX import and PDF export. If your problem is not dialogue but architecture - long stories, multiple storylines, TV series - this is the free plan that answers it. Exporting a clean .fdx back out needs Pro.

WriterDuet - the best free co-writing. Three projects free. Real-time co-writing needs a Plus plan for the project owner - free users can share read-only, or write in a paid user's script. If you write with a partner who pays for Plus, start here.

Arc Studio - the best free taste of the cloud format. Two scripts free, with outlining and comments like Google Docs. PDFs go out with a watermark on the free plan.

Celtx - the most limited free tier on this list. One project, basic editor, PDF export. It works as a trial for writers considering the paid plans, or for a single short.

Fade In, Final Draft, Scrivener, Highland Pro - trials, not free tiers. Fade In's demo has no stated expiry but watermarks PDFs. Final Draft gives you 30 days of the full version. Find the app for you

Answer three questions and we'll show one main recommendation and one backup. The logic is the same as in the reviews above: your stage and way of working matter more than the brand.

How to choose: three rules

01

Export first, convenience second

Check that the app exports clean FDX or Fountain. That's your exit if you switch editors tomorrow or a studio asks for the file. No export, no purchase, however beautiful the interface.

02

Count the 3-year price

Final Draft at $199.99 one-time (regular price $249.99), Arc Studio at $99 + $99 + $99, and Fade In at $79.95 once - those are three different economics. A subscription pays off through collaboration; a one-time purchase pays off through long solo work.

03

Structure is a separate tool

If your drafts fall apart by act three, a new text editor won't help. You need a board, storyline and timing analytics - that's Clotho's strength, plus Beat Board in Final Draft and outlining in Arc Studio.

Frequently asked questions

Can I write a screenplay in Word or Google Docs?

Technically, yes - but you'll be managing indents, pagination, and revisions by hand, and the producer will still ask for FDX or a properly built PDF. Any app in this review, including the free WriterSolo and Clotho plan, removes that routine entirely.

What is FDX, and why does everyone talk about it?

FDX is Final Draft's file format, the de facto standard for exchanging screenplays. Whether an app can import and export FDX without breaking scenes is the main test of professional fitness. In Clotho, FDX import is on any plan; export is on Pro.

Is Final Draft required to sell a screenplay?

No. A studio needs a properly formatted script and, at the production stage, a file that opens in their pipeline. Fade In, Arc Studio, Clotho, and WriterDuet all deliver that FDX. Final Draft reduces friction, but it isn't a pass.

How is Clotho different from Final Draft in practice?

Final Draft is first and foremost a page editor and a production tool. Clotho is an editor plus a living map of the story: a board, character storylines, timing, and analytics that update with every edit. If the structure is already in your head, an editor is enough. If structure is the problem, Clotho gives you what Final Draft doesn't.

Which app is best for free?

For solo work - WriterSolo: no project limit. For structure - Clotho's free plan with the board and FDX import. To try the cloud format - Arc Studio for 2 scripts and WriterDuet for 3 projects. See the full comparison in Best free screenwriting software in 2026.

WriterDuet is the benchmark: real-time collaborative editing, comments, chat, and video calls; real-time co-writing needs a Plus plan or higher for the project owner - free users can share their scripts read-only, or co-write in a paid user's script (checked on writerduet.com on 2026-11-11).
